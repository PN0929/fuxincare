Disability Certification for Older Adults in Taiwan: Eligibility, Benefits & How to Apply

Understand eligibility, benefits, application steps and support options for a parent living in Taiwan.

In-depth guide · approx. 12 min readUpdated: November 2026閱讀中文版 ↗

Key points in 3 minutes

  • A diagnosis or age alone does not guarantee eligibility. Functional impairment must meet Taiwan’s assessment criteria.
  • Benefits are not automatic. Health insurance subsidies, transport, equipment and allowances have separate rules.
  • Long-term care can be assessed separately. Adults aged 65+ can ask for LTC assessment via 1966 without first obtaining a disability certificate.

First, understand three different systems

As parents age, memory changes, mobility limitations or increasing help with daily tasks can raise an important question: should we look into disability certification in Taiwan?

  • Disability certificate (身心障礙證明): an official determination based on medical assessment and applicable review, which may provide access to disability-related benefits.
  • Long-term care (LTC) eligibility: an assessment of care needs and functional limitations. Older adults do not necessarily need a disability certificate to access LTC services.
  • National Health Insurance catastrophic illness certificate (重大傷病證明): relates primarily to certain NHI copayment exemptions, and is not the same as disability certification.

Taiwan now uses the term disability certificate (身心障礙證明). The older term “disability handbook” (身心障礙手冊) is no longer the official name.

When should a family ask about an assessment?

The diagnosis alone does not establish eligibility. Medical professionals assess the type, extent and lasting impact of functional impairment under Taiwan’s criteria. These are situations worth discussing—not a list of automatically qualifying diagnoses.

Health conditionWhen to ask a doctor
Dementia or cognitive declineOngoing changes in memory, judgment, communication or daily activities, such as becoming lost or struggling to manage routine matters.
Effects of strokeSignificant and persistent weakness, mobility, speech or other functional problems despite treatment and rehabilitation.
Parkinson’s diseaseSlowness of movement, rigidity or balance problems that continue to affect mobility and everyday tasks.
Severe vision or hearing impairmentSubstantial impairment that remains after appropriate treatment or correction.
Severe degenerative joint diseaseSignificant, ongoing limitations in walking, standing or transferring despite treatment.
Chronic disease affecting organ functionPersistent, substantial functional impairment involving the kidneys, heart, lungs or other organs.

Does a diagnosis guarantee a certificate?

No. Two people with dementia can receive different decisions because their cognitive function and daily living abilities differ. Aging, slower walking, hypertension or diabetes alone do not establish eligibility.

In general, prolonged significant impairments that have not improved with active treatment—or are medically expected to cause substantial disability for at least a year—may warrant a conversation about assessment. Some categories have additional rules.

You do not have to wait until your parent is completely dependent. Ask their treating physician when functional changes become persistent; the final decision is made through Taiwan’s formal assessment and review process.

What support might a disability certificate provide?

Depending on the assessed disability, needs, household circumstances and local rules, a certificate can help families access the following support.

Eligible records are generally transmitted by the relevant subsidy authority. A premium subsidy does not mean all doctor visits, hospitalizations or medical bills are free.

Does the certificate unlock every benefit?

No. Some concessions are available upon showing a valid certificate, while allowances, equipment, care programs or transport assistance can require separate applications, functional assessments or means testing. Benefits may also have restrictions on overlapping payments.

Before buying an assistive device or renovating a home, check whether prior assessment or approval is required for any subsidy.

Five things to consider before applying

Open the topics below for details relevant to your family.

1. The certificate may need renewal

Under Article 14 of Taiwan’s disability rights law, a disability certificate normally has a validity period of up to five years. Some irreversible conditions may qualify for exemption from reassessment, although periodic needs reviews still apply. For a certificate with an expiry date, pay attention to the window within 90 days before expiry for reassessment and review.

2. Assessment takes time and effort

Applications typically involve documents, designated medical providers and government review. For someone with significant mobility or cognitive difficulties, travel can be tiring. In-home medical assessment is possible only under specified conditions; ask the local health authority rather than assuming all housebound applicants qualify.

3. Certain cash allowances cannot be combined

Some means-tested senior allowances, disability living allowances, older farmers’ benefits and other statutory payments may be subject to choose-one rules. Simply receiving a disability certificate does not automatically cancel an existing allowance. Confirm the current payments and eligibility before applying for another cash benefit.

4. Services may have separate qualifications or waiting times

For example, a disabled parking permit requires relevant mobility eligibility, not just a disability certificate. Accessible transport, assistive equipment and care services may also have additional conditions, local capacity or booking requirements.

5. Include your parent's wishes

Some older adults may be uncomfortable with the label of disability. Begin with what support would make everyday life easier, explain the options, and respect their preferences and privacy where they can express them.

They are separate assessments and may both be relevant to the same person.

Can a parent receive LTC without a disability certificate?

Yes. Adults aged 65 or over with qualifying care needs can request an LTC assessment independently of disability certification. When bathing, dressing, moving about or preparing meals becomes difficult, families can contact Taiwan’s 1966 long-term care hotline. The two systems can be considered in parallel; overlapping benefits may be restricted.

How to apply in Taiwan

These four stages provide an overview. The household registration location determines the relevant local authority; document requirements may vary.

01Local district office
02Medical assessment
03Review and needs assessment
04Certificate issued

Step 1: Apply through the local government

Apply through an appropriate district/township office in the city or county of household registration, or use the official online channel when eligible. Ask for the latest application form, proof of identity, photographs, and any authorization documents for a representative. The office provides the assessment form and information on designated medical facilities.

Step 2: Attend a designated medical assessment

Medical professionals assess relevant physical, cognitive and functional domains based on the applicant’s situation. A dementia diagnosis alone does not guarantee approval. Ask whether existing records and test results will be useful.

Step 3: Government review and needs assessment

The medical report is sent to the relevant authorities for review. Social welfare needs assessments may be completed alongside the medical assessment or at another stage, depending on the type of support and local procedures.

Step 4: Receive the decision

If approved, check the certified severity, expiry date and relevant notations. The certificate does not automatically enroll a person in every available benefit program.

Can the assessment take place at home?

Under Article 11 of the disability assessment regulations, certain applicants—such as those completely paralyzed and unable to leave bed, those requiring 24-hour ventilation or life-support equipment, people in prolonged severe coma, or those facing other exceptional difficulties accepted by the local authority—may qualify for an assessment at their residence. Ask the local health authority about eligibility and documentation.

Can family members overseas authorize someone in Taiwan?

Administrative applications may be submitted through an authorized representative with the appropriate authorization and identity documents. However, a representative cannot take the applicant’s place in the medical assessment.

For families living abroad, Fuxin Care Management can discuss assistance with application information, document preparation, communication with providers and local offices, and coordination of medical appointments or care arrangements, subject to the agreed service scope.

See the official disability services portal and confirm specific steps with the local authority before applying.
