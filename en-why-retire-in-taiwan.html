RETIREMENT IN TAIWAN · ENGLISH

Why Retire in Taiwan? 6 Reasons to Consider Moving Back

From accessible healthcare to everyday convenience, here's why Taiwan deserves a place on your retirement shortlist.

After spending decades in the United States, Canada, Australia, or elsewhere, the idea of retiring in Taiwan can be appealing. Perhaps you grew up here and want to return to familiar surroundings. Maybe you still have relatives in Taiwan, or you simply miss the food, language, and everyday life.

But nostalgia alone isn't enough to make a retirement decision.

A good place to retire should make daily life manageable, offer access to healthcare, and provide opportunities to stay active and connected as you grow older.

Taiwan has several advantages worth considering, especially for people who have lived here before. Here are six reasons why moving back might be a good fit.

1. Healthcare Is Accessible, With an Established National Insurance System

Healthcare is often one of the biggest concerns when planning retirement.

Taiwan has a National Health Insurance (NHI) system that covers a broad range of medical services for eligible residents, including outpatient visits, hospital care, and many prescription medications.

In cities such as Taipei, you can find neighborhood clinics, pharmacies, and major hospitals within relatively short distances. This can make routine medical appointments easier to manage, particularly for retirees with ongoing health needs.

However, returning to Taiwan does not automatically mean you can use NHI immediately.

For Taiwanese nationals re-establishing household registration, enrollment depends on factors including previous NHI participation and employment status. Some returning residents may have to wait six months before becoming eligible.

Before moving, confirm your insurance status and make arrangements for any medication or medical care needed during a possible coverage gap.

2. You Can Enjoy Daily Life Without Depending on a Car

For retirees who no longer want to drive, Taiwan's transportation network can be a major advantage.

In Taipei and parts of New Taipei City, the MRT, buses, and taxis make it possible to handle many daily activities without owning a vehicle. Taiwan's High Speed Rail also connects major cities along the western corridor.

This can make visiting relatives, attending medical appointments, and enjoying short trips more convenient.

However, transportation varies considerably by neighborhood.

An apartment five minutes from an MRT station offers a very different experience from a hillside property where every outing requires a car or a long walk.

If you're planning to retire without driving, test the actual route from your potential home to the supermarket, nearest clinic, and public transportation stop before choosing a location.

3. Everyday Essentials Can Be Close to Home

One of Taiwan's appealing qualities is the variety of services available within residential neighborhoods.

Depending on where you live, you may find traditional markets, supermarkets, pharmacies, convenience stores, restaurants, and neighborhood clinics within walking distance.

For retirees, this can make ordinary tasks less demanding.

Instead of planning a long shopping trip, you might be able to pick up groceries, buy lunch, and stop by a pharmacy during a short walk around your neighborhood.

Traditional markets also offer a way to keep familiar routines, particularly for people who enjoy cooking Taiwanese food at home.

Of course, convenience comes with trade-offs. Central neighborhoods may have more services but also greater traffic, noise, and higher housing costs.

When comparing homes, consider what you'll actually need each week rather than simply choosing the area with the most attractions.

4. Staying Active and Social Can Be Part of Everyday Life

Retirement is about more than finding a comfortable apartment. It's also about having meaningful ways to spend your time.

Taiwan offers parks, walking paths, community centers, adult education programs, and activities for older residents.

For someone returning after years abroad, joining a class or visiting a neighborhood activity center can be a way to reconnect with the community.

You might enjoy morning walks, learning a new skill, meeting old friends, or exploring different parts of the island.

Taiwan also has community-based senior programs, although availability and eligibility differ by municipality.

If social connections are important to you, look beyond the property itself. Find out whether the surrounding neighborhood has places and activities you'd genuinely enjoy visiting regularly.

5. Retirement Can Offer More Housing and Lifestyle Choices

Taiwan offers different living environments within a relatively compact geographical area.

You might prefer a central Taipei neighborhood with convenient public transportation, a quieter residential area near a park, or a smaller city where daily life follows a different pace.

Housing costs vary significantly by location, building age, size, and accessibility features.

Rather than assuming Taiwan will always be cheaper than living overseas, compare the total cost of your preferred lifestyle.

Include rent or property expenses, building management fees, utilities, healthcare, transportation, and any assistance you may need later in retirement.

An older apartment without an elevator may cost less today, but it may become difficult to live in as mobility changes.

For people returning after many years abroad, renting first can be a practical way to explore neighborhoods before committing to a property purchase.

6. Long-Term Care Resources Are Available as Needs Change

Even if you're healthy and independent today, it's sensible to think about the kind of support you might need ten or twenty years from now.

Taiwan has a government-supported long-term care system that includes services such as home care, community-based programs, transportation assistance, assistive equipment, and respite care.

Access to subsidized services depends on eligibility and an assessment of care needs.

Families can also explore private services when they need assistance beyond what public programs provide, such as arranging appointments, coordinating household support, or managing ongoing care arrangements.

This can be particularly relevant when adult children live overseas.

Having a plan for local support can make it easier to remain in a familiar home while adapting to changing needs.

What Should You Check Before Moving Back?

Taiwan has much to offer, but a successful retirement move requires more than choosing a city you like.

Before making a final decision, consider these questions:

  • Residency: Do you have Taiwanese citizenship, household registration, or another valid basis for long-term residence?
  • Health insurance: When will you become eligible for NHI, and how will you cover medical needs before enrollment?
  • Housing: Would you prefer to rent first, and is your future home suitable for aging in place?
  • Finances: How will your retirement income, taxes, banking, and insurance arrangements work across countries?
  • Family support: Who could help locally if you needed medical appointments, household assistance, or emergency support?

For foreign nationals without an existing right of residence, retirement alone should not be assumed to provide a pathway to long-term residency in Taiwan. Immigration requirements must be checked separately.

It may also help to spend an extended period in Taiwan before committing to a permanent move. Living here for a few months can reveal much more about your preferred neighborhood and daily routine than a short holiday.

Is Taiwan the Right Place for Your Retirement?

For many people with personal or family ties to Taiwan, returning can mean reconnecting with a familiar culture while enjoying convenient transportation, accessible neighborhood services, and a range of healthcare resources.

Yet the right retirement location will depend on your health, finances, residency status, preferred lifestyle, and family circumstances.

A good starting point is to choose a few neighborhoods, estimate your living costs, confirm healthcare arrangements, and consider what support you might need in the years ahead.

If you have family members overseas, discussing these plans early can also help everyone understand how your retirement life in Taiwan might work.

A comfortable retirement begins with finding a place where everyday life feels manageable, enjoyable, and sustainable.
