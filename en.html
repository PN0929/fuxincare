RETURNING TO TAIWAN FOR CARE

Considering Surgery or Medical Care in Taiwan?

Taiwan is home to major medical centers with experienced clinical teams, efficient hospital systems and international-patient services. For self-pay patients comparing options across countries, Taiwan may be worth considering for its combination of medical quality and comparatively reasonable costs.

Orthopedic care is one common example. Knee replacement and hip replacement are established procedures offered by major medical centers in Taiwan. Fuxin does not provide medical advice or recommend a specific procedure — our role is to help coordinate the practical care journey around treatment already arranged with your hospital.

EXAMPLE CARE JOURNEY

Returning from the U.S. to Taiwan for Hip Replacement Surgery

Here is an example of how one international care journey could work. Every medical plan and recovery timeline is different, so actual arrangements would be customized around the hospital, treating team and patient’s mobility needs.

Meet Ms. Lin, 68, living in California

Ms. Lin plans to return to Taiwan for a scheduled hip replacement. Her adult children cannot take several weeks away from work, so the family contacts Fuxin before she flies to Taiwan.

01

Before She Flies

Fuxin speaks with Ms. Lin and her family by LINE or video call to understand the hospital, surgery date, expected stay, mobility needs and practical recovery arrangements.

  • Airport pickup planning
  • Accommodation before admission or after discharge
  • Hospital and appointment logistics
  • Caregiver coverage during hospitalization
  • Post-discharge daily support and follow-up transportation
02

Arrival in Taiwan

A pre-arranged vehicle picks Ms. Lin up at Taiwan Taoyuan International Airport and brings her to her accommodation. If her medical schedule and dietary instructions allow, we can also help her settle in with a familiar Taiwanese meal.

A warm bowl of congee, noodles or another familiar Taiwanese dish can make the first day back feel a little more like home.
03

Hospital Admission & Bedside Support

Hospital care in Taiwan may feel different from what some overseas patients are used to. Physicians and nurses provide medical care, while many patients also rely on family members, relatives or privately arranged caregivers for non-clinical bedside assistance.

  • Admission-day practical coordination
  • Caregiver scheduling during hospitalization
  • Meals, mobility, toileting and personal-care assistance within the caregiver’s permitted scope
  • Practical updates to overseas family members
04

Discharge — The Surgery Is Over, but the Care Journey Isn’t

Ms. Lin may not be ready to fly back to the United States immediately. She may need several weeks in Taiwan for recovery, follow-up appointments and rehabilitation ordered by her medical team. Her family home in Taiwan may also not be ideal for early recovery if it has stairs, a narrow bathroom, limited elevator access or insufficient space for a walker or wheelchair.

  • Short-term recovery accommodation with more accessible features when needed
  • Daily-living assistance
  • Coordination of suitable mobility aids or assistive equipment when recommended
  • Groceries, meals and practical errands
  • Transportation to follow-up and rehabilitation appointments
  • Family updates overseas
Why this matters: “Home” is not always the safest place immediately after discharge. We can help families look for a temporary recovery stay with practical accessibility features and, when appropriate, coordinate third-party mobility aids to better match the patient’s short-term needs.
05

Follow-Up Weeks in Taiwan

Week 1Hospital stay, bedside care and discharge planning.
Week 2Daily-living assistance, meals, transport and settling into recovery.
Week 3Follow-up visits and rehabilitation logistics.
Week 4Final practical arrangements and preparation to return home.

Illustrative timeline only. Recovery duration and fitness to travel must follow the treating physician’s assessment.

06

Returning to the United States

Once Ms. Lin’s treating physician confirms that travel is appropriate, Fuxin can help coordinate the final stage of her stay.

  • Airport transportation
  • Luggage and departure-day assistance
  • Coordination of airline wheelchair or airport assistance services when needed
  • Assistance through accessible public and check-in areas
  • Family notification that she has safely reached the airport
